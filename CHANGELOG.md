# Changelog

What changed for users in each Ketchup release. The full notes, downloads, and checksums are on the
[GitHub releases page](https://github.com/pietrobb/Ketchup/releases).

## Unreleased

- Fixed: two drilled boards overlapping by 50 × 100 × 18 mm passed `ketchup-program check` with exit 0 and only a `collision_unverified` warning, though their holes take out at most about 1 000 mm³ of the 90 000 mm³ the boards share. Two boxes of one orientation that share more than their holes, pockets and subtracted tools can remove are now a collision, with the least volume they share. The two parts of an `alternatives()` choice are now related as `alternatives`, and the halves of a split as `removed`; both were `boxes_only`, which says the exact solids were measured and do not overlap.
- Fixed: a bare contact between two grounded parts that the brace's force was not exactly across or along held it rigidly every way. A column between two grounded blocks bearing bare on its ends, everything turned 0.2° in plan, held the brace at 3.64 N/mm, turned 60° at 11.4, where the column holds 0.665 its weak way. The contact now carries only the force's part along its normal, and the rest goes the way the parts hold without it, here k / cos² β (0.665 to 2.66 N/mm). A grounded wall standing on a grounded slab with no `joint()` holds a brace nowhere, since the bare contact holds no slide; the refusal said the wall reaches the floor through nothing besides the member, and now names the wall and the slab and says to fasten them with `joint()`. A wall on a slab with no joint between them, which passed before bare contacts held only a push, is not verified for that reason.
- Fixed: past 2 000 overlapping pairs left for the exact check, the collision search stopped, so a real collision further on passed and `ketchup-program check` exited 0. The search now goes on without listing those pairs, skipping only pairs that the exact solids alone could settle and whose boxes do not already share more than their holes, pockets and subtracted tools can remove, and the collision fails the check. A pair the check did not list is now related as `unchecked`, not `boxes_only`, and relations past 20 000 overlaps add a `relations_truncated` warning.
- Fixed: a brace's path to the ground buckled at the timber's characteristic strength, held against the design force, so a slender brace up to about 1.6 times too weak passed. It now buckles at the design strength k_c f_c,0,d A, with k_mod / γ_M of the part that buckles at the load duration of the combination that sets the force (0.615 for C24 at medium term). The report and the basis of the calculation say so.
- Fixed: two grounded parts touching with no `joint()` still held each other along their contact: a block against a column's face, pulled along it, held the brace at 184 N/mm instead of the 0.665 N/mm the column holds alone. A bare contact now holds only a push across it, and the refusal names the two parts and says to fasten them with `joint()`.
- Fixed: a brace's joint to its anchor was measured from the middle of where they touch, not from the fasteners joining them, so the structural check could still pass a beam on a brace screwed once off its line. A plate on one screw 140 mm off the line of the member's screw held the beam at 215 N/mm instead of about 13 to 28, and a strip with a screw at each end buckled only up to the middle of its contact, holding 283 N instead of 186. The joint is now measured from the fasteners.
- Fixed: a brace on one screw, or on screws in one spot, counted as clamped whenever the screw sat within the parts' thickness of the force's line, so the structural check passed a beam braced by a plate whose one screw sat off that line, at 194.8 N/mm. The brace now knows the normal of its contact and turns about the screw unless the moment about that normal is negligible; the same plate holds about 29 N/mm and the beam is refused.
- Fixed: a brace buckled at the k_mod of the combination with the largest moment, so adding a small imposed load could turn a refused brace into a pass. Every ultimate combination now checks its own design force at its own k_mod, and the check and the calculation name the combination and k_mod closest to buckling. A brace of a strength class that names no product leaves the member not verified, as a member of that class does, instead of taking solid timber's k_mod.
- Fixed: almost every drawing with a text was saved as an underlay that Kečup 0.3.0 to 0.3.3 open as editable but draw blank. Such a file is now marked so those releases refuse it as newer; a file whose underlays are lines alone opens in them as before.
- Fixed: a bought part inside a `component()` could be applied only once; every later apply, even from the program's file, failed with "apply the program from its file". Documents saved before bought parts were named by their path (a part in a subfolder, `./x.step`, another letter case on Windows) can be changed again. An apply that replaces the document without a folder no longer keeps the previous document's folder, and a STEP file is read and hashed once per apply however many parts of it the program places.
- Fixed: fills and lines added to a DXF drawing could be owned by a group or a hatch instead of model space, so a second export drew the earlier room fills over the walls. They are owned by model space, and a drawing exported that way before keeps its order. The plan export to DXF and DWG is metric (`$MEASUREMENT 1`), so line types and hatches load from the metric files, and a draw order table added to a drawing has its class record.
- Fixed: an underlay from a DWG lost the architect's draw order: everything in the drawing's draw order table came out on top, so a hatch sent to the back covered the walls. The DXF of a DWG now keeps the whole order.
- Fixed: `str()`, `repr()`, the message of `fail()` or of a missing key, and a dict key of a value nested about 200 000 deep crashed the program check with a stack overflow and no answer; the value is now written with `...` past the nesting limit, and a deep key fails with its line. `enumerate`, `sorted` and `reversed` over a huge `range` are refused with "program memory limit exceeded" instead of crashing.
- Fixed: the printed structural calculation gave forces in kN to two decimals, so a brace refused for holding 259 N against the 263 N it must take read "0,26 kN" against "0,26 kN". Forces and loads now print to the newton ("0,259 kN" against "0,263 kN").
- Fixed: the room fills written into an architect's DXF were drawn over the walls and room names in AutoCAD, though they came first in the file. AutoCAD and ezdxf draw by handle, and a fill has the newest handle. The export now puts every fill under the drawing's own entities in model space's draw order (`ACAD_SORTENTS`), keeping the order the drawing already had, so in DXF 2000–2007, which has no transparency, a light grey room no longer hides the lines and texts inside it. Copies and lines drawn in Kečup stay on top. A drawing sheet exported as DXF or DWG is now metric in `$MEASUREMENT` as well as in its units, so line types and hatch patterns loaded into it later come from the metric files: ANSI31 was 3.175 mm apart and showed as a solid area on a 1:50 plan.
- Fixed: a program applied without its file's folder (a document opened again, `set_params`, `patch`, an MCP `apply` with `source` alone) kept the old body of a bought part when the program named another `part=` of the file or another folder, and a new part from a file of the same name in another folder took that body too, so collisions, exports and drawings used the wrong part without a warning. A part now takes the document's body only when it was read from the same path as the same assembly part; otherwise the apply is refused with "apply the program from its file". The folder an apply gave is no longer kept when the window opens or starts another document, so a program of the same file name there does not read the first document's files. A file replaced by other bytes of the same size and time of change (`cp -p`, `rsync -t`, an unpacked archive) is read again; before, the old body was sealed for it.
- Fixed: a program that nested values deeply while it ran stopped the process with no answer: `json.encode` of a list put inside a list a thousand times, or a list put inside a list 600 000 times with nothing else. In the window the program worker died and the edit failed with `worker_failed`. `json.encode` now refuses a value nested more than 200 deep with an error at its line, and the interpreter's memory cleanup handles any depth.
- Fixed: a check run before a bought part's STEP file was read took the part as a weightless point. A steel block 100 × 80 × 100 mm in `self_weight()` lying on a beam left the beam with no load and nothing missing, so the beam passed, and the only sign was a false `floating_part` warning. Such a part is now listed in `loads.unassigned`, and every member names it as missing and is not verified; it is no longer reported as floating. Once read, a bought part weighs the volume its file measures, not its box, which for a hinge is three to four times more.
- The CAM G-code dialects now say what their end moves assume of the machine. The ISO dialect, now named "ISO 6983 metric G-code with FANUC-style G43 and G28", follows ISO 6983 only in its word format: `G43 H` and `G28` have their FANUC meaning, and on a control that reads them otherwise, such as Heidenhain ISO where `G28` mirrors the part, it needs a dialect of its own. The LinuxCNC dialect's `G53 G0 Z0` expects Z homed to machine 0 at the top of its travel. Each dialect's sources say so, as does the AI's description of `cam_dialect`.
- Fixed: CAM programs in ISO, FANUC and LinuxCNC G-code cancelled the tool length (`G49`) with the tip still at the safe height, a few millimetres over the work. A controller that moves to cancel the length ran the tip that length lower, about 92 mm into the part with a 100 mm tool. The program now sends Z to the machine's reference point first (`G91 G28 Z0` then `G90` on ISO and FANUC, `G53 G0 Z0` on LinuxCNC) and only then cancels the length.
- Fixed: a check run before a bought part's STEP file was read did not say that the part's touches and collisions went unchecked: a hinge set into a door board gave no collision, and `ketchup-program check` exited 0. The report now has an `import_unread` warning naming the part and its file, lists no relation of it and exits 3. The headless check builds the rest of the program and names the part among what its exact check did not evaluate, where before it refused to build at all. The AI's `program check` with `source_path` reads the files beside the `.star` file as `program apply` does, so a new bought part is no longer refused there; the check keeps no folder for later applies.
- Fixed: a part `purchased(..., part=...)` took from an assembly file lay at the file's origin, not where the assembly places it, so a supplier's fitted hinge set came out about 900 mm away and its centre hinges upside down, and a program had to copy every placement out of the file by hand. The part now sits where the assembly places it, every placement from the assembly down to the part counted, turns included, and `at`, `x` and `z` move it with the file as a whole, as for a whole file. A part the assembly places more than once is refused as not saying which; name one of its placements. The placement is saved with the document (format 121), so after the document is opened again the part stays where it was without reading the file.
- A CAM program's result now says that the room under a through hole is not checked against the table, spoilboard or clamps: the drill goes past the stock's bottom by its tip and 1 mm, and only fixtures declared in the plan are checked there.
- An analysis joint whose faces' meshes meet only along a line is refused with the reason: the faces touch along an edge, or one covers only part of the other and neither mesh has nodes where the other face ends. Tied, the line would act as a hinge. Before, such a joint was refused as one whose faces do not touch over an area, which did not say what to change.
- The Viewer's Measure tool holds the points it snaps to once for each mesh, not once for each copy of it on screen. A package with many copies of the same part, as the drawn-index limit of 48 M allows, could hold about 1.7 GB of them after the pointer had passed over every copy, too much on a phone.
- Fixed: a column, post or wall on the path that holds a member sideways was taken to bend only, not to shear, so a part deep for its length held the member more stiffly than it does. A column 400 mm deep and 2 400 mm tall held a beam at 214.9 N/mm instead of about 194, and a wall-like part 1 200 × 2 400 at about 2.2 times its stiffness. Every part on the path now shears as braces already did.
- A check stopped at its 60 s deadline now says why and what to change, on the command line and in the window alike: the program and its checks share that time, and the usual causes are long loops and many parts in one place or close together. The window's program worker, stopped by that shared deadline, said the program alone ran past the few seconds left and to shorten its loops; it now names the check's deadline. A worker that does not answer within 30 s of starting ran no program, so it is now reported as `worker_failed` saying so, not as the check's deadline with advice to change the program. `docs/program-language.md` lists `time_limit` among the exit-2 cases.
- A program can place bought parts from STEP files: `purchased("hinge 1", "hettich/9237903.stp", "9237903", "Hettich", at = (18, 0, z), z = (-1, 0, 0), x = (0, 0, 1))` puts the file's exact body where the parameters say, so a taller door moves its hinges. The document stays the program's; before, importing the hinge's STEP detached the program and a second copy of the model with the hardware had to be kept by hand. The path is relative to the program's file and may not leave its folder. `part=` takes one part of an assembly file, by its name or by the name of its placement, as supplier assemblies often name every part "SOLID". Each file is read once however many parts use it and is kept in the document, so set_params and Push/Pull do not read it again; after the document is opened again, an unchanged file's bodies, an assembly's parts too, come from the document and its assembly is not read again either. Once a file's bytes change, it is read again, the part `part=` names in it too, both in the open window and after the document is opened again. Bought parts are listed in the hardware list by supplier and article with their count, never in the cut list, woodWOP, BTLx or CNC, and take no holes or cuts. Bought parts take part in the collision check with their exact bodies and are exported to STEP, GLB and the Viewer with the rest. A file that cannot be read is refused naming the part, the path and why. `imported(name, file)` places a STEP body that is not bought. The AI's `program apply` with `source_path` reads the files beside that `.star` file; an apply by source text alone uses the bodies the document already holds, so a headless apply without a file path cannot add a part from a new file.
- Fixed: the structural check could pass a beam held sideways by a wide brace on one screw at each end. The brace was taken to push along its own length, while it turns about its screws and pushes along the line through them: a plate 300 × 1000 × 40 mm screwed off its axis to a grounded post 20 × 400 mm held the beam at 214.9 N/mm and passed, and now holds about 24 N/mm against the 56 asked.
- Fixed: comparing a later revision of an underlay's drawing took a text set in another font, and a polyline the architect redrew wider, as unchanged, as an import kept neither. A text now keeps its style's font file and family and the fonts its MTEXT codes switch to, and an LWPOLYLINE its constant width, or else its widest vertex. A change in either is reported as a change in style; a text style renamed with the same font is not. Neither is drawn yet. An underlay that keeps them is saved in a newer form of its drawing, which Kečup 0.3.3 and older cannot draw.
- Fixed: the structural check could pass a beam whose brace pulls a grounded part off another grounded part it only rests on. Two grounded parts touching with no `joint()` were taken as one rigid part, so a column bearing on a block held a brace pulling it away from the block at 247 N/mm; it holds only what the column holds alone, about 0.6 N/mm, at any angle between the pull and the contact. A bare contact still holds where one part pushes the other, and a brace whose force finds no path to the ground holds nothing; before, the beam was checked as braced. The basis of the calculation says so.
- Fixed: past a relative slenderness of 0.3, a piece on a brace's path to the ground buckled at Euler's load alone, up to 11 times its crushing load (1412.6 kN against 240 kN at 0.49). It now buckles at k_c f_c,0,k A by EN 1995-1-1 6.3.2. The basis of the calculation also says that the path runs through each part without the moment of its distance from the part's centre, that only the stiffest path to the ground is taken, and that a `diaphragm()` is a rigid sheet on the springs of its fasteners.
- Fixed: the cap on a `thermal_check` air layer's `r` took the column for heat flowing down for every element, so a wall passed 0.19–0.23 m²K/W where EN ISO 6946 Table 8 gives 0.18. It now follows the way heat flows through the element, as the surface resistances already did. Advice for a wall where vapour condenses and the site is missing names the missing site too.
- Fixed: a document changed outside Kečup and sealed again, with a group, occurrence, definition, tag or classification dimension filed under another id than its own, closed Kečup while opening or opened editable and closed the window at its first frame. Such a document is now refused when it is read, in every older format as well.
- Fixed: a shared Viewer package that places one large mesh tens of thousands of times passed every size limit and froze the Viewer. The triangles of every copy now count, and a package that would draw more than 48 million indices is refused.
- Fixed: `ketchup-program check` and the window's program check could run for minutes on a program that puts many parts in one place, as a loop that forgets to move them does: 5000 overlapping boards ran past 300 s. Overlapping pairs and listed collisions stop at a limit and the report says the list is cut short, and the command-line check answers by its deadline. Parts are paired only when they are near on all three axes, not on one: 5000 boards stacked in a column took 20.7 s and now take 0.19 s. Pairs whose boxes overlap but whose solids only the exact shapes settle no longer count toward the 100 listed collisions, so a house of turned rafters is not reported as more than 100 overlaps.
- Fixed: a program nesting a few thousand levels deep, such as a list in a list or a sum of thousands of terms, crashed `ketchup-program` with no answer and killed the window's program worker. It is now refused as a syntax error on its line, and the worker stays for the next program. Starting a program worker takes only the time the check's deadline has left, and a real out-of-memory is reported as such rather than as a timeout.
- Saving a document keeps its permissions and its symbolic links: a read-only or group-shared file no longer comes back with the default permissions, a link to the document stays a link, and the `.recovery` file gets the document's permissions beside the link's target.
- The MCP server answers a JSON-RPC batch, a bare number or string, and an id past the 64-bit range, which got no reply at all. Its line limit follows the window's 8 MiB request instead of 32 MiB, and waiting calls have a byte budget besides their count, so sixteen large calls can no longer fill memory.
- A failed export now lists in the Connect tab every sheet and companion file it could not put back, and its `.work-recovery` copy; a refusal because someone else changed the file is no longer listed as an AI write. The advice to set HOME is given only where it helps. In a container whose `/proc/self/cgroup` cannot be read, the memory limit is read from the v1 root.
- A workshop package packed again by another tool is refused with that reason and asks for the package as Kečup wrote it. A memory budget under 1 MiB is given in KiB, not as 0 MiB, and a heat conduction study counts its model check against the FEA memory budget.
- The Measure tool no longer fills the face under the pointer. Measuring picks points and edges, so the fill only jumped from face to face while aiming; the snap marker still shows the point that a click takes. Orbit, Pan and Zoom Window no longer fill it either. Select, Push/Pull and the other tools that act on a face or part still show it.
- The Connect tab's list of what an AI wrote keeps the last 100 writes, not the last 100 files, so one drawing set of many sheets no longer pushes every earlier write off the list. The old sheets a set removed are listed as removed, and the set's answer to the AI names them. A file an export changed and then could not put back after failing is listed too. On Linux, an empty or relative `XDG_CONFIG_HOME` is ignored, so an AI can never write into `~/.config/autostart`. When a folder on the way to the home folder is replaced by a link or a file while Kečup reads it, the message says to try again instead of telling the user to set HOME.
- Fixed: `ketchup-program check` passed rounds and chamfers OCCT then refused. Each edge was checked on its own, so r = 6 on both edges of a 10 mm face passed; the finishes on one face now add up and must stay below its width, and the refusal gives the real limit (9.99 mm, not "less than 10 mm"). A finish too small to build names its parameter (`radius must be more than 0.01 mm, got 0.005`). A surface or Loft input the document refuses names its feature, and the Assistant names the operation of its program and the field, with that field's rule as the repair. A SketchUp model with more stretched copies than one import stores says so, not that the file has too many vertices. In a cgroup v1 container the memory limit is read from the process's own group, not only the root.
- The structural check no longer gives a stocky part on a brace's path to the ground a buckling force it would crush long before: a 200 × 400 × 200 mm block bent by a hanger showed F_cap 29 035.77 kN. A piece whose relative slenderness is at most 0.3 (EN 1995-1-1 6.3.2) now holds without buckling, shown as "bez vzperu"; slender braces and anchors buckle as before.
- An FEA study needs less memory and says more when it is too large. Its matrices kept a place for every element's share of a value, about six per value on tetrahedra, and the memory budget counted them so: a bar of 5760 tetrahedra needed 32 MiB to solve and 68 MiB for its natural frequencies, now 22 and 29. Checking the model and a heat conduction study are counted against the same budget before they take the memory. A study refused as too large says it would need at least the size given, for frequencies as for a solve, and a model with too many elements, materials, constraints, loads or couplings names each limit instead of nodes alone. Natural frequencies called converged say that the finest mesh still overstates them. An assembly study gives, for each mesh level, how many nodes of glued faces no tie holds (`untied_bonded_nodes`), such as the nodes of an edge two glued faces share when the part is finer than one neighbour and coarser than the other.
- Fixed: a `thermal_check` air layer could be given more resistance than its thickness allows. Every `r` up to 0.23 m²K/W was accepted, the value of a 300 mm layer; the limit now follows EN ISO 6946 Table 8 by thickness, so 5 mm of air takes at most 0.11 m²K/W. A wall in which vapour condenses, with every input given, no longer advises to "give what is missing"; it advises a vapour control layer on the warm side or the annual moisture balance of EN ISO 13788. `ketchup-program check --summary` shows the thermal checks (`thermal`: verdict, counts and the build-ups not verified), so a summary that exits 3 for a thermal check says why.
- A CAM arc that strays from its chord by at most half the controller's step is now always written as a straight `G1`. A very flat arc, such as radius 2500 mm over 0.0011 mm, turns so little that a controller may run its `I`/`J` as a full circle; it was written as an arc because its rounded ends stayed apart. A refused arc now names its operation, its motion and where it starts, and a drill whose point cannot make a blind hole's floor names the operation and the hole's X and Y. The export's list of what it does not check now says that a written arc may differ from the planned one by up to four steps, not half a step. The workshop package's `losses.csv` is described for programs that read it: every field is quoted, and one starting with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return is written after an apostrophe.
- Fixed: the structural check could pass a beam held sideways by a thin brace butted end to end against a thicker anchor. The brace buckled only over its own length, so a 15 × 15 mm brace 1200 mm long on a 30 × 30 mm anchor 1200 mm long held 277.6 N against the 263 N asked, while the two buckle together at about 112 N. Every piece of a path pushed along its length now buckles over the whole length of those pieces end to end, up to the part that takes the force across: the brace above holds 69.4 N, on the safe side, and the beam is no longer passed.
- Fixed: the structural check could pass a beam held sideways by a brace whose anchor does not hold it. The anchor was asked only how it holds a force across the beam where it meets the brace. A brace pinned at its ends now pushes its anchor along the brace, so a grounded post 20 mm thick, bent its weak way by a strut at 40° in plan, holds the beam at 0.94 N/mm and no longer at 124.8 N/mm. A brace fixed at both ends now turns its anchor about the point of the beam it holds, so a post twisted by a brace that meets it 425 mm along the beam is counted with that twist. Two screws turn against their slip only about the normal of their contact. Under a moment about an axis lying in the contact, the joint turns freely, since that would pull the screws out and EN 1995-1-1 table 7.1 gives no slip modulus for it: two screws along that axis no longer fix a hanger's top. A deck butting a wall therefore no longer anchors a diaphragm to it; a deck screwed down onto a grounded plate or wall still does. A diaphragm held through one anchor alone, or through anchors whose paths share a part, turns that anchor about the point of the beam as a fixed brace does: a deck on one plate over a post 20 × 400 holds the beam at 1.3 N/mm, no longer 198.6, and a deck on a single end wall is held weakly far from that wall. Anchors apart from each other hold the deck as a rigid sheet on them: anchors on both sides of the point of the beam, such as a wall under each end, add up, while anchors on one side hold the deck's turn only by their spacing, so two plates 220 mm apart on posts 20 × 200 beside each other hold the beam at 3.7 N/mm, no longer 58.4.
- Fixed: an FEA study of parts glued where one is meshed finer than the other came out too soft. A part glued in more joints than the other was tied by the other's nodes alone, so the nodes of its finer face between them stayed free: a shelf glued between two sides meshed four times coarser sagged 79 % more than one body. The finer face's nodes now follow the coarser face, and a glued edge two joints share is still tied once. Tested: shelves meshed 0.5 and 0.67 mm between sides meshed 1 and 2 mm sag within 0.2 % of one body.
- Fixed: a CAM program could cut a full circle into the part. An arc whose two ends fall on one point after rounding to the controller's step, though it turns less than a full turn, was written as `G2`/`G3` with `I` and `J`, which FANUC, Grbl and LinuxCNC run as a full circle; one such block cut about 6 mm into the part. An arc whose rounded words no longer describe it is now written as a straight `G1` when that line stays within half a step of the arc; otherwise the export is refused and says which arc and what to change. The program's list of what it does not check names the rounding to the controller's step, that no coolant is switched on (no `M7`/`M8`) and that every plunge goes straight down, with no ramp or helix.
- Fixed: a blind hole was drilled short of its depth by the length of the drill's point. `BORE Ø6.8 mm DEPTH 18 mm, 118° POINT` reached its full diameter only at about 16 mm, a conical floor was not drilled at all, and the simulation counted the difference as stock left on purpose. The point now goes down until the full diameter reaches the bottom of the wall, and a drill whose point cannot make the hole's floor, such as a 118° drill in a flat-floored hole, is refused. A rectangular pocket now finishes its end walls, where its zigzag left ridges of 0.32 mm. Peck drilling no longer feeds the whole way down from the safe height for every peck: each peck rapids to 1 mm above the depth already drilled.
- Fixed: the structural check could pass a beam held sideways by braces that do not hold it. A brace screwed to its anchor with one screw counted as clamped there; it is now a pin, and a brace pinned at an end only pushes along its length, so a 50 × 100 × 600 mm hanger on one screw no longer holds a beam and a slanting pinned brace is no longer about four times too stiff. Anchors whose paths to the ground share a part count once, so more anchors on the same post add nothing. A brace's buckling length runs on through its joint into the anchor: two 1200 mm rods bolted end to end buckle as one 2400 mm rod. A part that is only bent sideways is no longer given a buckling force of about 1 MN. `ketchup-program check --summary` counts the joints beside the members (`statics.joints`), so it no longer reads "every member passes" beside a verdict its joints left not verified.
- Fixed: `ketchup-program check` exited 0, as for a pass, when a thermal check was not verified, for example when Glaser finds vapour condensing in the winter state; it now exits 3, as statics does. A `thermal_check` layer given an `r` above 0.23 m²K/W, the most an unventilated air layer has by EN ISO 6946, is refused with the advice to give its material by λ. Errors in λ, μ and r no longer say "within … mm".
- Fixed: opening or importing a SketchUp or GLB file with many copies of a component, each scaled a little differently, could close the window out of memory. The placed triangles are counted before the stretched copies are made, and the import stops with the reason once the copies alone pass the most one import stores; nothing that imported before is refused.
- The acceptance check of a received workshop package passes only what Ketchup writes. A header with extra fields, such as a Unicode Path field that makes an unpacker show another name than the stored one, a header flag other than the UTF-8 name, a comment on the package or on an entry, which an unpacker shows the operator, and an entry marked as anything but the plain DOS file Ketchup writes are refused; a symbolic link marked as made on VMS, Atari, BeOS or AtheOS passed and was unpacked as a link. In the package's `losses.csv`, a field that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return is written after an apostrophe, so a spreadsheet does not run it as a formula.
- Fixed: the natural frequencies of an FEA study were called converged from its finest mesh alone. They are now compared with the mesh level before it, with the relative change, and the answer names what they leave out: the mesh, rigid supports, no damping and no prestress. A part glued to another on two neighbouring faces is solved; it failed on a node tied twice. Faces that touch only along an edge, at a right angle or side by side, are no longer glued into a hinge. ## 0.2.0 — 2026-10-18

- Every value field reads values the same way: the system decimal separator, units, and arithmetic. Scale has grips on the selection box.
- Sketch, Push/Pull, Move, Rotate, and Scale each make one Undo step; lines drawn on a body lie on its face.
- Tapped holes use shop tap drills and the DIN 76-1 runout. Mirrored parts get their own BOM row and drawing. Timber members are checked on their real section, or reported as *not verified*.
- Rule programs and DWG reading run in a separate process with memory and time limits. STEP and IGES export run in the background with Cancel.
- Plugins run from a hashed private copy; a file from a newer Ketchup is refused instead of losing data.
- Ketchup Viewer picks faster; on Android it opens packages of 120 MB and recovers an interrupted save.

Files from 0.1.0 open in 0.2.0. A file saved by 0.2.0 may need 0.2.0 to open.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-10-14

First public release: an open-source 2D/3D parametric modeler for Windows with exact geometry, rule programs, production exports, Ketchup Viewer, and AI editing over MCP that goes through the same validation and Undo as a manual edit.
